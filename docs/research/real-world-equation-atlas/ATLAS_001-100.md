# Real-World Equation Atlas — v0.1

**Purpose:** a practical, evidence-oriented index of mathematical models used across real-world systems.  
**Status:** research scaffold. Equations below are canonical starting forms, not universal prescriptions. Each deployment requires units, assumptions, conditions, parameter identification, and validation.  
**Selection principle:** choose the simplest model that meets the application's accuracy, safety, uncertainty, interpretability, and compute requirements; compare alternatives rather than declaring a universal winner.

## How to read entries
Each entry gives a canonical form, core use, and critical qualification. Before engineering use, define every symbol and unit, establish initial/boundary conditions, test dimensional consistency and limiting cases, and validate against independent observations.

## A. Optimization, estimation, and decision science

1. **Constrained optimization** — minimize (f(x)) subject to (g_i(x)\leq0, h_j(x)=0). Use: resource allocation, design, scheduling. Requires feasible-set definition and justified objective; local methods may find only local optima.
2. **Linear programming** — minimize (c^Tx) subject to (Ax\leq b, x\geq0). Use: production, transport, blending. Exact for linear objectives/constraints; nonlinear effects require another model.
3. **Quadratic programming** — minimize \(\tfrac12x^TQx+c^Tx\) subject to linear constraints. Use: portfolio approximations, control, least-squares design. Convex global optimum when \(Q\succeq0\).
4. **Least squares** — minimize \(\|Ax-b\|_2^2\). Use: calibration, regression, reconstruction. Classical assumptions matter; outliers and correlated/nonconstant noise can distort results.
5. **Regularized least squares (ridge)** — minimize \(\|Ax-b\|_2^2+\lambda\|x\|_2^2\). Use: ill-conditioned estimation and prediction. Choose \(\lambda\) by held-out validation or principled inference.
6. **Lasso** — minimize \(\|Ax-b\|_2^2+\lambda\|x\|_1\). Use: sparse models and feature selection. Correlated predictors can make selected features unstable.
7. **Bayes' rule** — \(p(\theta\mid D)=p(D\mid\theta)p(\theta)/p(D)\). Use: inference under uncertainty. Results depend on likelihood, prior, and model adequacy.
8. **Maximum likelihood** — \(\hat\theta=\arg\max_\theta p(D\mid\theta)\). Use: parameter estimation. Check identifiability, sampling assumptions, and uncertainty.
9. **Robust optimization** — \(\min_x\max_{u\in\mathcal U} f(x,u)\). Use: decisions under bounded uncertainty. Uncertainty set \(\mathcal U\) must be defensible; overly broad sets can be excessively conservative.
10. **Multi-objective/Pareto optimization** — minimize vector \((f_1(x),\ldots,f_k(x))\). Use: balancing cost, performance, safety, and emissions. Report trade-off frontier; weights are value judgments.

## B. Linear algebra, geometry, and numerical foundations

11. **Linear system** — \(Ax=b\). Use: circuits, structural models, balances, network flows. Check rank, conditioning, units, and existence/uniqueness.
12. **Eigenproblem** — \(Av=\lambda v\). Use: vibration modes, stability, principal components. Eigenvalues have meaning only relative to the operator and boundary/model assumptions.
13. **Singular value decomposition** — \(A=U\Sigma V^T\). Use: compression, denoising, inverse problems. Truncation trades detail for stability/compression.
14. **Principal component analysis** — eigenvectors of covariance matrix \(\Sigma\), or SVD of centered data. Use: dimensionality reduction. Linear variance directions need not preserve task-relevant structure.
15. **Graph Laplacian** — \(L=D-A\). Use: network diffusion, clustering, connectivity. Weighted/directed graphs need appropriate Laplacian variants.
16. **Barycentric coordinates** — \(p=\sum_i\lambda_i v_i,\ \sum_i\lambda_i=1\). Use: finite elements, interpolation, geometric modeling. For points inside a simplex, weights are nonnegative.
17. **Rigid transformation** — \(p'=Rp+t\), with \(R^TR=I\) and \(\det R=1\). Use: robotics, CAD, computer vision. Frame conventions and multiplication order must be explicit.
18. **Homogeneous transform** — \(T=\begin{bmatrix}R&t\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\). Use: chained robot/camera frames. Distinguish active motion from passive coordinate change.
19. **Least-squares state reconstruction** — \(\hat x=\arg\min_x\|Hx-y\|^2\). Use: sensor fusion and tomography. Observability/identifiability depend on H.
20. **Condition number** — \(\kappa(A)=\|A\|\|A^{-1}\|\). Use: assess sensitivity of linear solves. Large condition number signals amplification of input/roundoff error.

## C. Dynamics, control, and robotics

21. **State-space model** — \(x_{k+1}=Ax_k+Bu_k+w_k,\ y_k=Cx_k+v_k\). Use: dynamic systems and control. Linear approximation and noise model must be checked.
22. **Kalman filter** — predict \(\hat x^-_k=A\hat x_{k-1}+Bu\); update \(\hat x_k=\hat x^-_k+K_k(y_k-C\hat x^-_k)\). Use: linear-Gaussian state estimation. Covariances and model assumptions govern performance.
23. **Extended Kalman filter** — linearize nonlinear dynamics/observation about current estimate. Use: nonlinear tracking/navigation. Linearization can fail under strong nonlinearity.
24. **PID control** — \(u(t)=K_pe(t)+K_i\int e(t)dt+K_d\dot e(t)\). Use: industrial motion/temperature/flow control. Requires tuning, saturation handling, and stability validation.
25. **LQR** — minimize \(\sum_k(x_k^TQx_k+u_k^TRu_k)\) under linear dynamics. Use: optimal feedback control. Depends on model accuracy and positive cost matrices.
26. **Hamiltonian mechanics** — \(\dot q=\partial H/\partial p,\ \dot p=-\partial H/\partial q\). Use: conservative mechanical systems. Dissipation/constraints require extensions.
27. **Lagrangian mechanics** — \(\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q}=Q_{nc}\). Use: multibody dynamics. Generalized coordinates and nonconservative forces must be defined.
28. **Robot manipulator dynamics** — \(M(q)\ddot q+C(q,\dot q)\dot q+g(q)=\tau\). Use: robot arm simulation/control. Identify parameters and validate friction, compliance, and actuator limits.
29. **Inverse kinematics** — solve \(f(q)=x_{target}\), often minimizing pose error plus regularization. Use: robot positioning. Multiple/no solutions and singularities are common.
30. **Rigid-body attitude (quaternion)** — \(\dot q=\tfrac12\Omega(\omega)q\). Use: 3D orientation tracking. Normalize quaternion and state convention; avoid treating quaternion coordinates as four independent physical dimensions.

## D. Continuum physics, heat, fluids, and electromagnetism

31. **Conservation law** — \(\partial_tu+\nabla\cdot F(u)=S\). Use: mass, momentum, energy, species transport. Flux and source terms encode the specific physics.
32. **Heat equation** — \(\partial_tT=\alpha\nabla^2T+Q\). Use: thermal diffusion. Assumes continuum conduction and often constant properties; boundary conditions dominate solutions.
33. **Wave equation** — \(\partial_{tt}u=c^2\nabla^2u\). Use: acoustics, strings, elastic waves. Constitutive assumptions determine c and valid frequency range.
34. **Poisson equation** — \(\nabla^2\phi=-\rho/\epsilon\) (electrostatic convention). Use: electrostatics, gravity analogues, potential fields. Sign and constants depend on convention/medium.
35. **Laplace equation** — \(\nabla^2\phi=0\). Use: source-free steady potentials. Boundary data determine the solution.
36. **Navier–Stokes** — \(\rho(\partial_tu+u\cdot\nabla u)=-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2u+\rho f\), with incompressibility \(\nabla\cdot u=0\) where applicable. Use: fluid flow. Turbulence and closure/meshing are major challenges.
37. **Fourier heat flux** — \(q=-k\nabla T\). Use: conductive heat transfer. Fails for some nanoscale/ultrafast/nonlocal regimes.
38. **Fick's law** — \(J=-D\nabla c\). Use: molecular diffusion. Effective D and non-Fickian transport need validation.
39. **Maxwell–Faraday law** — \(\nabla\times E=-\partial_tB\). Use: induction and electromagnetic devices. Part of coupled Maxwell system, not a complete standalone field model.
40. **Maxwell–Ampère law** — \(\nabla\times H=J+\partial_tD\). Use: electromagnetic wave/device modeling. Material constitutive relations and boundary conditions are essential.

## E. Signals, information, and computation

41. **Fourier transform** — \(X(f)=\int x(t)e^{-i2\pi ft}dt\). Use: spectral analysis and filtering. Sampling/windowing and transform conventions matter.
42. **Nyquist–Shannon sampling** — bandlimited signal with sampling rate \(f_s>2f_{max}\) can be reconstructed ideally. Use: digitization. Real anti-alias filters and finite records require margins.
43. **Convolution** — \((x*h)(t)=\int x(\tau)h(t-\tau)d\tau\). Use: linear time-invariant systems and imaging. LTI assumptions and boundary effects matter.
44. **Z-transform** — \(X(z)=\sum_nx[n]z^{-n}\). Use: discrete-time system analysis. Region of convergence determines stability/causality.
45. **Shannon entropy** — \(H(X)=-\sum_xp(x)\log p(x)\). Use: uncertainty/information. Depends on probability model and log base.
46. **Mutual information** — \(I(X;Y)=\sum_{x,y}p(x,y)\log\frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}\). Use: dependence and feature selection. Estimation is difficult in high dimensions.
47. **Bayesian filtering/HMM** — \(p(x_t\mid y_{1:t})\propto p(y_t\mid x_t)\int p(x_t\mid x_{t-1})p(x_{t-1}\mid y_{1:t-1})dx\). Use: tracking and sequence inference. Transition/emission models must fit reality.
48. **Error-correcting code bound (Hamming)** — code distance d detects up to d−1 errors and corrects up to floor((d−1)/2). Use: reliable storage/communication. Actual code construction determines achievable rate.
49. **Algorithmic complexity** — classify resource growth, e.g. \(T(n)=O(n\log n)\). Use: algorithm selection. Asymptotics omit constants, memory, and hardware behavior.
50. **Numerical stability/error propagation** — perturbations obey bounds of form \(\|\delta x\|/\|x\|\lesssim\kappa(A)\|\delta b\|/\|b\|\). Use: solver reliability. Bound depends on norm and problem conditioning.

## F. Materials, structures, and manufacturing

51. **Linear elasticity** — \(\sigma=C:\varepsilon\). Use: small-strain material response. Constitutive tensor and elastic regime must be appropriate.
52. **Strain-displacement relation** — \(\varepsilon=\tfrac12(\nabla u+\nabla u^T)\). Use: continuum structural mechanics. Small-deformation assumption.
53. **Static finite elements** — \(K u=f\). Use: structures, thermal systems, fields. Mesh convergence, element quality, and boundary conditions must be checked.
54. **Modal analysis** — \(K\phi=\omega^2M\phi\). Use: resonance and vibration design. Linearized model; damping may need separate treatment.
55. **Buckling eigenproblem** — \((K+\lambda K_G)\phi=0\) (sign convention varies). Use: structural stability. Imperfections and nonlinear post-buckling require more complete analysis.
56. **Fatigue S–N relation** — empirical stress-life curve, often \(N=C(\Delta\sigma)^{-m}\). Use: cyclic life estimates. Material, surface, environment, and load spectrum matter.
57. **Fracture mechanics** — \(K_I=Y\sigma\sqrt{\pi a}\). Use: crack-growth/criticality. Geometry factor and fracture toughness are material/condition specific.
58. **Thermal expansion** — \(\Delta L=\alpha L_0\Delta T\). Use: tolerances and thermal design. Linear approximation over limited temperature ranges.
59. **Darcy's law** — \(Q=-(kA/\mu)(\Delta p/L)\). Use: porous media flow. Validity depends on laminar porous-flow regime and permeability.
60. **Additive manufacturing energy density** — proxy \(E_v=P/(vht)\). Use: process-window exploration. Not a universal quality law; calibrate against material, machine, scan strategy, and measurements.

## G. Chemistry, biology, and medicine

61. **Mass-action kinetics** — \(\dot c_i=\sum_r\nu_{ir}r_r(c)\). Use: reaction networks. Rate laws and parameters require experiments.
62. **Michaelis–Menten** — \(v=V_{max}[S]/(K_m+[S])\). Use: enzyme kinetics. Quasi-steady-state and substrate assumptions apply.
63. **Logistic growth** — \(\dot N=rN(1-N/K)\). Use: bounded population growth. Constant carrying capacity and homogeneous mixing may be unrealistic.
64. **Lotka–Volterra** — \(\dot x=\alpha x-\beta xy,\ \dot y=\delta xy-\gamma y\). Use: idealized predator–prey dynamics. Real ecosystems need additional mechanisms/data.
65. **SIR epidemic model** — \(\dot S=-\beta SI/N,\ \dot I=\beta SI/N-\gamma I,\ \dot R=\gamma I\). Use: simple epidemic dynamics. Contact heterogeneity, behavior, latency, and interventions may require extensions.
66. **Reaction–diffusion** — \(\partial_tu=D_u\nabla^2u+f(u,v)\), \(\partial_tv=D_v\nabla^2v+g(u,v)\). Use: pattern formation and spatial biology. Pattern claims require parameter and stability analysis.
67. **Gene regulation ODE** — \(\dot x_i=f_i(x,\theta)-\delta_ix_i\). Use: gene-expression dynamics. Inferred networks need causal/experimental validation.
68. **Pharmacokinetic one-compartment model** — \(\dot C=-(CL/V)C+\text{input}/V\). Use: concentration-time approximations. Multi-compartment, nonlinear, and patient-specific behavior may be needed.
69. **Allometric scaling** — \(Y=aM^b\). Use: biological scaling hypotheses. Exponent is empirical and domain-dependent; avoid extrapolation without evidence.
70. **Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium** — genotype frequencies \(p^2,2pq,q^2\), with \(p+q=1\). Use: population-genetic baseline. Assumes random mating and no evolutionary forces in idealized form.

## H. Networks, infrastructure, and operations

71. **Network flow conservation** — \(Bf=d\). Use: water, power, logistics, communication. Incidence matrix and sign conventions must be consistent.
72. **Shortest path** — minimize \(\sum_{e\in path}w_e\). Use: routing and navigation. Weights must reflect time/cost/risk; dynamic networks need updates.
73. **Queueing utilization** — \(\rho=\lambda/\mu\) for a single-server queue. Use: capacity planning. Stability requires \(\rho<1\) for standard M/M/1.
74. **M/M/1 mean time** — \(W=1/(\mu-\lambda)\). Use: simple service systems. Exponential/Poisson assumptions and steady state are strong.
75. **Reliability series system** — \(R_{sys}=\prod_iR_i\) for independent components all required. Use: system reliability. Independence assumption often fails.
76. **Availability** — \(A=MTBF/(MTBF+MTTR)\). Use: maintainability planning. Assumes stable long-run rates and clear failure/repair definitions.
77. **Inventory EOQ** — \(Q^*=\sqrt{2DS/H}\). Use: basic replenishment sizing. Deterministic demand/lead-time assumptions; ignores many real constraints.
78. **Critical path** — project duration is maximum path sum in precedence network. Use: schedule analysis. Resource constraints and uncertainty need extensions.
79. **Power balance** — \(P_{in}=P_{out}+P_{loss}+dE/dt\). Use: energy systems and circuits. Define system boundary and sign conventions.
80. **Economic input-output model** — \(x=Ax+d\), hence \(x=(I-A)^{-1}d\) when invertible. Use: sectoral production dependencies. Fixed coefficients and invertibility/feasibility conditions matter.

## I. Finance, risk, and statistical decision

81. **Compound growth** — \(V_t=V_0(1+r)^t\). Use: deterministic growth scenarios. Constant rate assumption; not a forecast guarantee.
82. **Continuous compounding** — \(V(t)=V_0e^{rt}\). Use: continuous-time growth/discounting. Rate convention and cash-flow timing matter.
83. **Present value** — \(PV=\sum_t CF_t/(1+r)^t\). Use: project valuation. Discount rate and cash-flow scenarios dominate results.
84. **Portfolio variance** — \(\sigma_p^2=w^T\Sigma w\). Use: portfolio risk aggregation. Covariance estimates are unstable and regime-dependent.
85. **CAPM** — \(E[R_i]=R_f+\beta_i(E[R_m]-R_f)\). Use: benchmark expected-return model. Empirical limitations; not a guaranteed predictor.
86. **Value at Risk** — \(VaR_\alpha(L)=\inf\{l:P(L\leq l)\geq\alpha\}\). Use: loss quantiles. Does not describe tail severity beyond quantile.
87. **Conditional VaR** — expected tail loss beyond VaR under a defined convention. Use: tail-risk optimization. Estimation depends on distribution and data regime.
88. **Bayesian decision rule** — choose \(a^*=\arg\min_a E[L(a,\theta)\mid D]\). Use: decisions under uncertain states. Loss function encodes priorities.
89. **Survival model (Cox)** — \(h(t\mid x)=h_0(t)e^{\beta^Tx}\). Use: time-to-event analysis. Proportional hazards and censoring assumptions must be checked.
90. **Regression calibration** — \(y=X\beta+\epsilon\). Use: prediction/explanation. Check residual structure, confounding, leakage, and out-of-sample performance.

## J. Modern geometry, spacetime, and advanced model families

91. **Metric distance** — \(ds^2=g_{ij}dx^idx^j\). Use: geometry, relativity, manifold learning. Metric signature and coordinate chart must be specified.
92. **Geodesic equation** — \(\ddot x^\mu+\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}\dot x^\alpha\dot x^\beta=0\). Use: shortest/stationary paths on curved spaces and free-fall idealization. Connection/metric and parameterization matter.
93. **Einstein field equation** — \(G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}/c^4\). Use: classical gravitational field model. Requires stress-energy specification and regime interpretation.
94. **Lorentz transformation** — \(x'=\gamma(x-vt),\ t'=\gamma(t-vx/c^2)\). Use: inertial-frame coordinate transformation in special relativity. Standard form assumes relative motion along x.
95. **Four-vector invariant** — \(s^2=c^2t^2-x^2-y^2-z^2\) (one signature convention). Use: spacetime interval. Sign convention must be explicit.
96. **Floquet theory** — periodic linear system \(\dot x=A(t)x,\ A(t+T)=A(t)\); stability from monodromy matrix eigenvalues. Use: periodic systems and time crystals. Periodicity and linearization are required.
97. **Tensor transformation** — \(T'^{i_1\ldots}_{j_1\ldots}=\frac{\partial x'^{i_1}}{\partial x^{a_1}}\cdots\frac{\partial x^{b_1}}{\partial x'^{j_1}}T^{a_1\ldots}_{b_1\ldots}\). Use: coordinate-independent physical laws. Index variance and chart mappings must be handled consistently.
98. **Geometric algebra product** — \(ab=a\cdot b+a\wedge b\) for vectors. Use: unified rotations/orientations and geometric computation. Algebra signature and grade interpretation must be stated.
99. **Lattice cryptography (LWE form)** — samples \((A, As+e\bmod q)\) with secret s and error e. Use: post-quantum cryptographic constructions. Security relies on parameterized hardness assumptions; do not substitute toy parameters for standards.
100. **Equivariant representation** — \(f(g\cdot x)=\rho(g)f(x)\). Use: symmetry-aware ML, molecular geometry, physical modeling. Group action and representation must match the task.

## Cross-domain structural analogies
- Optimization ↔ energy minimization ↔ shortest path: all compare candidate states under a cost, but their feasible spaces and physical meanings differ.
- Linear systems ↔ network flow ↔ circuit laws: all use balance equations; incidence/constitutive structure determines the system.
- Eigenvalues ↔ vibration modes ↔ graph diffusion ↔ stability: spectra describe how operators act on characteristic modes.
- Fourier analysis ↔ eigenfunctions of translation-invariant operators: frequency is a natural mode for homogeneous linear systems.
- Conservation PDEs ↔ chemical reaction networks ↔ population models: local rates plus fluxes/sources govern state change.
- Coordinate transforms ↔ robot frames ↔ tensor laws: distinguish changing representation from changing the physical object.
- Bayesian inference ↔ Kalman filtering ↔ state estimation: repeated updating of uncertain beliefs from observations.
- Reaction–diffusion ↔ biological patterning ↔ heat transport: spatial coupling can create smoothing or pattern-forming behavior depending on reaction terms.

## Prioritization recommendation
For a general-purpose engineering/research system, develop these first: (1) constrained optimization, (2) linear algebra/least squares, (3) conservation laws/PDEs, (4) state-space estimation/control, (5) Fourier/sampling, (6) Bayesian uncertainty, (7) graph Laplacians, (8) eigenvalue/spectral methods, (9) finite elements, (10) reaction networks. This is a practical portfolio recommendation, not a universal ranking.

## Required validation for every promoted entry
1. Dimensional/unit check.
2. Assumption and regime checklist.
3. Boundary/initial-condition review.
4. Limiting-case and invariance tests.
5. Parameter identifiability and uncertainty.
6. Numerical convergence/stability where applicable.
7. Held-out or independent experimental validation.
8. Sensitivity/robustness analysis.
9. Independent reproduction.
10. Formal proof only for claims that are mathematical and formalizable; clearly separate proof from empirical validation.

## Evidence labels
- **ESTABLISHED:** accepted mathematical/physical framework within stated assumptions.
- **REPRODUCED:** specific result independently reproduced with artifacts.
- **FORMALLY_VERIFIED:** theorem/proof checked in a proof assistant or rigorous derivation.
- **COMPUTATIONAL_CONJECTURE:** supported by computation, not proven.
- **UNVERIFIED:** plausible but insufficient evidence.
- **REFUTED:** contradicted by evidence or proof.

This atlas is a map for research, not a substitute for domain-specific engineering standards or professional review.
