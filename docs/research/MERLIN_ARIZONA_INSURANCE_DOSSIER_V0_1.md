# Arizona insurance research dossier — v0.1

**Status:** source-grounded starting dossier; not a carrier ranking, legal opinion, quote, coverage determination, or live license check.

**Research boundary:** Arizona personal and commercial P&C are in the stated agency research scope. California, Florida, Georgia, New York and Texas remain personal P&C only; other jurisdictions remain research-only unless authority is separately verified.

## 1. Regulatory and licensing baseline

- Use the Arizona Department of Insurance and Financial Institutions (DIFI) producer pages and live License Search for individual and business-entity authority. A research record must not be treated as proof of a producer's current license, line, appointment, or insurer authority.
- DIFI's current producer licensing page describes resident/nonresident individual and business-entity applications and states that resident producers with a major line of authority generally complete 48 CE hours per four-year period, including at least six ethics hours. Recheck the live page and the actual license record before relying on this.
- Keep separate records for individual producer, business entity, DBA, line of authority, nonresident authority, appointment, surplus-lines authority and renewal/CE status.

## 2. Personal auto: minimums are not adequate-limit advice

DIFI and ADOT currently describe Arizona's minimum financial-responsibility limits as **25/50/15**: $25,000 bodily injury per person, $50,000 bodily injury per accident, and $15,000 property damage. The statutory minimum is a compliance floor, not a needs-based recommendation.

Research checklist:
- Compare liability limits, UM/UIM selection and limits, medical payments, collision/comprehensive, rental, glass, towing, OEM parts and rideshare/business-use endorsements.
- Inspect household/family or intra-family exclusions carefully. DIFI warns that some forms may limit bodily-injury liability for household members to the statutory minimum even where higher limits were purchased.
- Test named-driver/excluded-driver terms, permissive use, youthful operators, delivery/rideshare, business use, garaging, territory, vehicle additions/replacements and notice requirements against the exact policy form.
- Capture credit-based insurance scoring and loss-history/C.L.U.E. disclosures separately from the policy contract; document correction/dispute routes.
- Compare DIFI premium and complaint-ratio materials only with their scenario assumptions, dates, denominators and stated limitations. A complaint ratio is not a finding that an insurer acted unlawfully.

## 3. Homeowners, renters and condominium

DIFI describes common forms including HO-3, HO-4, HO-6 and manufactured-home coverage. Replacement cost is not market value; ACV reflects depreciation. Compare the declarations, base form and every endorsement.

Arizona exposure and wording checklist:
- Wildfire/brush, monsoon wind, hail, roof age/material, dust, water backup, surface-water flood, earth movement, heat-related losses, vacancy and protection safeguards.
- Dwelling replacement-cost estimator assumptions, inflation guard, extended/guaranteed replacement cost, ordinance or law, debris removal and matching.
- Roof settlement schedules, cosmetic damage restrictions, percentage wind/hail deductibles, ACV roof endorsements and actual repair/replacement conditions.
- Water backup versus flood; seepage, repeated leakage, mold/fungi/rot; service lines; equipment breakdown; refrigerated property; additional living expense.
- Personal property special limits, scheduled property, replacement-cost contents, identity/cyber endorsements and personal liability.
- Condo unit-owner responsibilities versus HOA master policy, loss assessment, walls-in definition, building ordinance and deductible assessments.
- Renters: personal property valuation, additional living expense, liability, roommate/household definitions, sublimits and off-premises property.

Do not infer coverage from a product label or DIFI consumer guide. The policy form and endorsements govern the contract.

## 4. Flood, earthquake and specialty layers

DIFI explains that standard homeowners coverage generally excludes flood and that flood/earthquake may require separate coverage. For each quote, compare NFIP versus private flood terms, building/contents limits, waiting periods, basement restrictions, replacement-cost eligibility, loss-of-use availability, community eligibility and lender requirements. Preserve the actual quote date and form edition. Distinguish surface flood, sewer backup, groundwater, seepage and plumbing discharge; these are not interchangeable causes of loss.

## 5. Claims conduct and complaint evidence

A.R.S. §20-461 identifies specified unfair claim-settlement practices where performed with sufficient frequency to indicate a general business practice. Its text includes misrepresentation of policy facts, unreasonable communication/investigation practices, certain refusals to pay without reasonable investigation, and failure to attempt fair settlement where liability is reasonably clear. The statute states that it supplies an administrative remedy to the Director and does not create a private right of action. Do not generalize its legal test to other states.

For each conduct-research entry, record:
- exact insurer legal entity and line/state;
- source type (statute, regulator finding, court ruling, complaint aggregate, consumer allegation, or marketing);
- date range, denominator, disposition and insurer response where available;
- whether the record establishes a fact, allegation, contractual design, or unresolved hypothesis.

## 6. Carrier and contract comparison schema

No company should be called “best” from a brand name, star rating, or one complaint statistic. A comparable row requires:
1. exact issuing legal entity and NAIC company code;
2. Arizona authorization/admitted or eligible surplus-lines status;
3. exact policy form and endorsement identifiers, editions and effective dates;
4. same risk facts, limits, deductibles, valuation and quote date;
5. exclusions, sublimits, settlement terms and relevant endorsements;
6. state/line complaint data with denominator and context;
7. exact-entity financial evidence and dated rating opinion;
8. market-conduct findings and claims-service evidence;
9. price, fees, payment plan and distribution compensation;
10. availability and renewal evidence.

If any material dimension is missing, mark the comparison **incomplete / unranked**. Surplus-lines eligibility does not establish solvency or claims quality; disclose that guaranty-association protection generally does not apply to surplus-lines policies.
