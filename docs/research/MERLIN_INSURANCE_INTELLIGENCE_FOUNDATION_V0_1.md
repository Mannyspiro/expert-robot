# Merlin Insurance Intelligence — Foundation v0.1

## Purpose

Create a source-grounded learning and research system covering insurance science, P&C product construction, underwriting, distribution, regulation, claims, reinsurance, actuarial methods, consumer protection and international comparisons. This is educational/research software, not legal advice, policy advice, claims adjusting, underwriting or a substitute for licensed professionals.

## Agency scope

Preserve the previously defined business scope:
- Arizona: personal and commercial P&C.
- California, Florida, Georgia, New York and Texas: personal P&C only.
- All other jurisdictions: research and education only unless the user separately establishes licensing and appointments.
This is not a live license or appointment verification. Before any transaction, verify the producer, business-entity, line, nonresident, appointment and surplus-lines authority.

## Sherlock-style knowledge method

Every fact is a typed record with proposition, jurisdiction, line, product, form edition, effective date, source type, publication/retrieval date, permitted-use metadata, hash where retention is allowed, confidence, counterexamples and unresolved questions.

Evidence types include statutes, regulations, regulator bulletins, filed forms, endorsements, court decisions, market-conduct reports, complaint aggregates, financial filings, actuarial studies, academic research, marketing statements and consumer testimony.

Evidence states distinguish verified fact, documented practice, contractual design, allegation, industry pattern, disputed matter, historical context and unverified hypothesis. Never turn one anecdote into a universal claim. Preserve counterexamples and source context.

Research cycle: observe → classify → source → compare → infer → challenge → verify → teach → update.

## Doctoral-level curriculum

1. Risk theory: pooling, frequency/severity, adverse selection, moral hazard, insurability, diversification, utility, ruin theory and capital.
2. Actuarial science: GLMs, credibility, survival analysis, reserving, IBNR, loss development, trend, catastrophe models and reinsurance.
3. Insurance economics: underwriting cycles, distribution economics, commissions, reinsurance costs, capital allocation and risk transfer.
4. Contract interpretation: insuring agreements, definitions, exclusions, conditions, endorsements, causation, subrogation, contribution, bad faith and jurisdictional differences.
5. Underwriting: appetite, eligibility, inspections, valuation, catastrophe zones, lawful rating variables, telematics and predictive models.
6. Claims: notice, investigation, causation, proof of loss, depreciation, replacement-cost holdback, mitigation, subrogation, appraisal and litigation.
7. Regulation: licensing, appointments, rate/form filing, solvency, market conduct, unfair trade practices, privacy, cancellation/nonrenewal and surplus lines.
8. Reinsurance and specialty markets: treaty/facultative, quota share, excess of loss, catastrophe layers, captives, RRGs, Lloyd's and delegated authority.
9. Agency operations: independent/captive/direct/wholesale, MGA/MGU, binding authority, broker-of-record, producer duties, E&O and documentation.
10. Consumer protection: clear coverage comparisons, disclosure, complaint rights, accessibility, conflicts and fair treatment.

## Product taxonomy

Personal lines: private passenger auto, homeowners, renters, condo, landlord/dwelling fire, manufactured home, personal umbrella, NFIP/private flood, earthquake, scheduled valuables, motorcycle, RV, watercraft, personal cyber, identity fraud, pet, travel and title.

Commercial lines: BOP, commercial package, commercial property, CGL, commercial auto, workers compensation, employers liability, umbrella/excess, professional E&O, D&O, EPLI, cyber, crime/fidelity, inland marine, builders risk, equipment breakdown, business interruption, contingent BI, cargo/ocean marine, pollution, product liability, liquor liability, garagekeepers, dealers open lot, surety, fiduciary, event cancellation, aviation and farm/ranch.

Each product dossier should include target exposures, available markets, actual forms and endorsements, coverage grants, exclusions, conditions, limits, sublimits, valuation, deductibles, waiting periods, underwriting evidence, claims workflow, state law, consumer comparison checklist, renewal triggers, carrier appetite and source provenance.

## High-impact contract review topics

Review actual policy language for:
- Actual cash value versus replacement cost and depreciation.
- Roof schedules, cosmetic damage and matching limitations.
- Water backup versus surface-water flood; flood and earth-movement exclusions.
- Ordinance/law, code upgrades, debris removal and construction costs.
- Mold, fungi, rot, repeated seepage, wear/tear and faulty workmanship.
- Vacancy, protective safeguards and notice/cooperation conditions.
- Coinsurance, margin clauses, agreed/stated value and underinsurance.
- Percentage wind/hail/storm deductibles.
- Sublimits, aggregates and defense costs inside/outside limits.
- Business-income waiting periods, restoration periods and dependent properties.
- Claims-made retroactive dates and extended reporting.
- Consent-to-settle, hammer clauses and panel counsel.
- Additional insured, primary/noncontributory and waiver of subrogation.
- Payroll/sales audits, classifications and subcontractor evidence.
- Cyber war/systemic event and social-engineering wording.
- Pollution, silica, asbestos, PFAS and microbial exclusions.
- Auto symbols, scheduled drivers, garaging and radius restrictions.
- Appraisal, arbitration, suit-limitation and cancellation/nonrenewal wording.

These are review topics, not proof of misconduct. Compare base form, every endorsement, schedules, application representations and notices. Marketing summaries alone are insufficient.

## Insurer comparison standard

Never create a universal best-company list. Compare separately by jurisdiction × product × customer/risk profile × exact issuing legal entity × policy form edition × effective date.

Required evidence:
1. Exact insurer legal entity and NAIC company code.
2. State authorization/admitted status or qualified surplus-lines eligibility.
3. Actual form numbers, editions, endorsements and filed status when available.
4. Coverage breadth, exclusions, sublimits, deductibles and loss settlement.
5. Comparable quote using identical limits, deductibles, exposures and dates.
6. Complaint index/reasons by state and line, with denominators and context.
7. Financial condition and rating-agency opinion for the exact issuing entity.
8. Market-conduct findings, enforcement actions and relevant litigation.
9. Claims-service and catastrophe-response evidence.
10. Compensation/distribution conflicts, renewal stability and availability.

Incomplete profiles are not rankable. Price is only one factor. A group rating is not a substitute for the issuing entity's rating. Surplus-lines eligibility is not a guarantee of financial condition or claims practices; explain the absence of state guaranty-fund protection where applicable.

NAIC Consumer Insurance Search has terms and data-completeness limits. Respect its personal/noncommercial use restriction; do not bulk copy, resell or republish proprietary data.

## Company conduct research

Study documented underwriting, pricing, renewal, claims, vendor and distribution practices. Each entry must distinguish lawful disclosed product design, common economic practice, regulator-criticized conduct, court-adjudicated conduct, allegation and unresolved hypothesis. Record the exact law, policy clause, source, jurisdiction and disposition.

For Arizona, A.R.S. §20-461 identifies specified claim-settlement practices prohibited when committed with frequency indicating a general business practice. Do not transfer Arizona's legal test to other states without verifying their statutes.

Build complete Arizona dossiers for:

- Auto financial-responsibility limits and UM/UIM.
- Homeowners, renters, condo, landlord and manufactured-home coverage.

- Producer licensing, business entities, appointments, CE, E&O and surplus-lines diligence, taxes and disclosures.
- Commercial exposures: contractors, trades, habitational, restaurants, retail, property owners, fleets, agriculture, hospitality, nonprofits and technology.

Verify current statutes and effective dates; do not rely on stale course notes for deadlines.

## 50-state plus DC framework

Represent all 50 states and DC. For each, retrieve and verify regulator links, producer lines and nonresident rules, appointments/CE, rate/form access, auto minimums and UM/UIM, cancellation/nonrenewal, claims standards, credit/telematics rules, workers compensation, guaranty association scope, surplus-lines eligibility/tax/disclosures, residual markets and complaint procedures.

Sequence: Arizona; California, Florida, Georgia, New York and Texas; then the remaining jurisdictions. NAIC charts are discovery aids; primary state law and regulator publications control.

## International and cultural notebook

Compare mutual/cooperative insurance, takaful, microinsurance, public social insurance, Lloyd's subscription market, captives, parametric cover, catastrophe pools, cross-border motor systems and civil-law/common-law contract interpretation. Record local communication norms, family/community risk-sharing, trust institutions, language, business etiquette and disaster-recovery customs. Attribute observations and avoid stereotypes; local law and individual variation take precedence.
