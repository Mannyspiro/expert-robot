ML and Agentic AI: improve a business process, not just a model

Scenario: a company receives 1,000 invoices each month. Your task is to design a helpful, efficient workflow that catches mistakes and sends consequential decisions to the right person.

1. First, understand the ML model

Past invoices
examples

Labels
routine / exception
→
Features
vendor, amount, PO

Train model
learn patterns
→
Test on new cases

Intuition: training is practice with examples and answer keys. Testing is the unseen exam. If the same examples leak into both, the score can look excellent without proving the model works on new invoices.

A model predicts or classifies. It does not, by itself, own the business process or permission to post a payment.

2. Now design the agentic workflow

Intuition: the model is one specialist in a team. The agent coordinates the steps, uses approved tools, checks results and stops for human approval when the action matters.
