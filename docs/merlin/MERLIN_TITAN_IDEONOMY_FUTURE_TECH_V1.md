# Merlin × Titan: Ideonomy future-technology research v1

**Status:** research hypotheses and engineering concept package; not validated products.
**Scope:** publicly documented science and engineering, original functional abstractions of science-fiction motifs, bounded low-voltage prototypes.
**Governance:** Capability grows, authority never expands.

## 1. Research question

Can Merlin systematically translate compelling fictional capabilities into physically plausible, independently testable engineering systems, then use Titan's CPU-first compute fabric to accelerate simulation, design-space exploration and evidence synthesis?

The answer is pursued as a research programme, not by assuming fictional devices work. A film or television reference is treated as a *function prompt* (what capability does the device provide?) rather than a design source. No protected character, proprietary visual design, exact fictional mechanism or dialogue is reproduced.

## 2. Ideonomy / FIND-N method

The first-wave catalogue is generated by a repeatable functional-decomposition process:

1. **Extract function:** express the fictional capability as an observable input → transformation → output.
2. **Decompose:** split it into sensing, actuation, energy, materials, computation, communication, control and human-interface primitives.
3. **Cross-domain map:** search for analogues in public research, engineering standards, open hardware and natural systems.
4. **Simplex recombination:** combine primitives from distinct domains (for example, event-driven computing + local work queues + evidence provenance) and retain only combinations with a coherent physical or computational model.
5. **Constraint pass:** apply conservation laws, material limits, energy balance, thermal limits, latency, manufacturing, cost, ergonomics and safety.
6. **Falsifiable prototype:** define the smallest experiment that could disprove the central claim.
7. **Evidence and independent review:** store source, assumptions, CAD, wiring, simulation output and test results as immutable artifacts; ask CACE to verify exact bytes, schemas and reproducibility.
8. **Iterate:** update candidate scores only when evidence changes.

The current scoring rubric is transparent triage, not proof of novelty or feasibility:

| Dimension | Weight | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Feasibility | 25% | Near-term physical/software plausibility |
| Testability | 20% | Can a small experiment falsify the claim? |
| Evidence | 20% | Strength and relevance of public research |
| Safety | 15% | Can early tests remain bounded and non-human? |
| Integration | 10% | Fit with Merlin, Titan, CAD, wiring and available tools |
| Novelty hypothesis | 10% | Interesting recombination, not patent novelty |

Scores are on a 0–10 scale. The weighted score ranks *what to investigate first*, not what is proven to work.

### 3.1 Titan Adaptive Compute Loom

**Cinematic function prompt:** a distributed ship computer that routes a task to the most suitable subsystem.

**Engineering translation:** a CPU-first heterogeneous compute fabric. Hermes decomposes research into typed tasks; Titan schedules bounded jobs across local CPU nodes; vectorized kernels accelerate appropriate numerical work; the shared event stream reports progress; CACE independently checks the output artifacts. The initial design is deliberately ordinary hardware and software. SIMD, multicore execution and task locality are measurable today. A GPU-like *programming model* or parallel workload is not a claim of GPU-equivalent performance.

Future branches may investigate neuromorphic or photonic accelerators as optional, independently benchmarked modules. Nature's 2025 neuromorphic review discusses scaling and ecosystem gaps; the 2026 photonic reservoir-computing paper reports a research platform, not a universal drop-in accelerator. The design therefore does not depend on either technology being commercially ready.

**Bench prototype:** two CPU compute nodes, a small Ethernet switch, fused low-voltage distribution, regulated rails, fan cooling, current/temperature sensing and one reproducible RK4 workload. Compare:
- task throughput and p50/p95 latency;
- joules per completed task;
- CPU utilization and memory movement;
- thermal steady state and throttling;
- recovery after one node is disconnected;
- reproducibility of result hashes.

### 3.2 Modular Autonomous Fabricator Cell

**Cinematic function prompt:** a workshop that turns a digital design into a physical object.

**Engineering translation:** a bounded desktop fabrication cell combining a conventional additive-manufacturing process, a low-force positioning arm, camera registration, fixtures and post-build inspection. NASA's public autonomous-assembly and laser-welding demonstrations establish relevant component capabilities, but do not establish a universal replicator.

**Prototype:** print a calibration fixture, identify it using fiducials, perform one bounded pick/place or probe operation, and measure placement error. CAD outputs include a frame, carriage, camera mount, tool plate and calibration grid. Wiring remains low-voltage with external certified power supplies, fused motor branches, limit switches and an emergency-stop input.

### 3.3 Soft-Tendon Assist Sleeve

**Cinematic function prompt:** wearable mechanical augmentation.

**Engineering translation:** a soft compliant support with tendon routing, force sensing and a current-limited motor spool. Recent artificial-muscle research uses ultrasound-addressed microbubble arrays to produce programmable deformation, but that is a research mechanism, not a ready-to-build wearable actuator.

**Prototype:** a dummy-joint bench fixture only. Measure torque, cable friction, travel, current, temperature, hysteresis and controller stability. No human-worn powered prototype is in this phase.

### 3.4 Spatial Engineering Table

**Cinematic function prompt:** an interactive room-scale spatial interface.

**Engineering translation:** a coordinate-consistent digital twin that unifies FreeCAD assemblies, electrical connectivity, simulation plots, provenance and tracked input. The near-term version is an interactive screen/AR workspace, not a free-space hologram.

**Prototype:** select a CAD part and highlight its electrical nets and simulation state; measure coordinate-registration error and interaction latency.

### 3.5 Field-Sense Multimodal Surveyor

**Cinematic function prompt:** a handheld instrument that rapidly characterizes an environment.

**Engineering translation:** replaceable, calibrated temperature, humidity, pressure, optical/IR and selected gas/particulate sensors with uncertainty-aware fusion. It reports measured values and confidence; it does not diagnose illness or claim to identify arbitrary substances.

**Prototype:** compare three sensors against reference instruments, characterize drift/noise and store calibration curves with each data artifact.

## 4. Titan architecture and wiring concept

Titan is a compute fabric, not a fictional energy source. The first physical prototype is designed around a certified external 12 V DC adapter, with a 5 A maximum input budget (60 W nominal maximum). No mains wiring is placed inside the enclosure.

Power branches in the wiring concept:
- 12 V input → 5 A main fuse → distribution bus;
- Node A: 2 A branch fuse → regulated 5 V rail, selected for the board;
- Node B: 2 A branch fuse → regulated 5 V rail, selected for the board;
- cooling: 0.5 A branch fuse → 12 V fans;
- telemetry: 0.5 A branch fuse → microcontroller and sensors.

These are initial budget targets, not final fuse or wire selections. Final ratings depend on measured inrush/steady current, conductor ampacity, connector ratings, regulator thermal dissipation and local requirements. Implement a documented return/ground strategy, strain relief, reverse-polarity protection and thermal shutdown. Use only an enclosed, certified external adapter; do not build or expose mains circuitry.

**CAD files:**
- `cad/titan/Titan_Compute_Loom_v1.scad`: parametric enclosure tray, generic compute-board envelopes, power-distribution plate, network switch and cable guides. Open in OpenSCAD; dimensions are generic and must be checked against selected boards.
- `cad/titan/Titan_Compute_Loom_v1_wiring.svg`: low-voltage power and data topology.
- Next CAD revision should add actual board STEP models, connector envelopes, cable bend radii, airflow simulation and a complete bill of materials after the target hardware is selected.

## 5. Merlin integration

The Ideonomy engine is deterministic and dependency-light:
- `seed_candidates()` defines the first-wave hypotheses;
- `build_ideonomy_report()` emits method, sources, scores, evidence and unknowns;
- `register_ideonomy_report()` stores the exact JSON bytes in the canonical artifact registry and creates Titan/candidate nodes in the knowledge graph.

The graph labels all candidates `unreviewed`. No score or generated design promotes a claim. The next runtime integration is to register this report as a Hermes research task, send each prototype plan to the numerical/CAD/electrical handlers, and pass exact resulting artifact bytes to CACE for independent schema/content checking.

Titan should initially schedule CPU work using the existing Hermes worker and bounded RK4 engine. Accelerator adapters are optional and must demonstrate measurable benefit on the same workload before they are accepted.

## 6. CAD, electrical and simulation deliverables per candidate

Every candidate must eventually carry:
1. A parametric source CAD file and neutral exchange export (STEP where supported).
2. An assembly tree with part identifiers, material assumptions, dimensions and tolerances.
3. A wiring diagram, netlist, connector/pin table, fuse and power budget.
4. A simulation model with solver identity, units, boundary conditions and input parameters.
5. Test protocol, measured data, uncertainty, failed runs and reproducibility instructions.
6. An image set and storyboard showing the concept, exploded assembly, wiring and prototype.
7. A CACE receipt bound to the exact source commit, artifact digest, schema and logical path.

A rendered concept image is not engineering validation. A CAD mesh is not a manufacturable drawing. A simulated circuit is not a certified design.

## 9. Immediate experiments and exit criteria

**Experiment T-01 — CPU fabric baseline**
- Run identical deterministic RK4 and graph workloads on one node and two nodes.
- Record wall time, p50/p95 latency, CPU time, peak memory, energy at the DC input and artifact digest.
- Pass only if distributed mode improves a predeclared metric without changing numerical outputs beyond a declared tolerance.
- If overhead dominates, keep the workload local and revise scheduling; do not claim acceleration.

**Experiment T-02 — thermal/power envelope**
- Measure branch currents, regulator temperature, board temperature and throttling at idle and sustained load.
- Stop on component-limit breach, unexpected heating, unstable supply or wiring/connector temperature rise.
- CACE checks the data schema and raw log digest; human engineering review determines safe operating limits.

**Experiment F-01 — fabrication registration**
- Print a fiducial fixture and repeat a low-force probe/pick-place cycle.
- Report absolute and repeatability error across at least 30 trials; preserve failed attempts.

**Experiment S-01 — sensor calibration**
- Compare each channel against a traceable or documented reference instrument over its intended operating range.
- Report bias, drift, noise and cross-sensitivity. Do not label a sensor calibrated without that comparison.

## 10. Boundaries

- All technology claims must cite public sources or be labelled as hypotheses.
- No fictional physics is treated as established fact.
- No unverified accelerator or power-source claims.
- No human-worn powered exosuit tests in this phase.
- No medical diagnosis or safety certification from the surveyor.
- No autonomous fabrication without operator approval, guarded motion and bounded work envelope.
- No hypothesis promotion from generated prose, CAD renders or simulation alone.
- No merge, deployment, external provider activation or paid infrastructure is implied by this research package.

## 11. Correction and authoritative Titan security track (2026-11-04)

**Correction:** Titan is the project's security/encryption system. The earlier description of Titan as a CPU-first compute fabric was a naming/context error. That compute-fabric concept is a separate Merlin research candidate and is not Titan's cryptographic architecture.

### 11.1 Historical baseline

Prior design notes describe an experimental rotor / N-pick-K / Steckerbrett / graph-topology research concept; a later AES-256-GCM + scrypt baseline with random salt/nonce and strict payload validation (historical report: 19 checks); and Titan envelope v1 using HKDF-SHA-256, domain-separated derivation, authenticated topology/context metadata and AES-256-GCM-SIV (historical report: 43/43 prototype checks). These counts are historical prototype tests, not proof of cryptographic security or independent review. Experimental graph/rotor ideas must not replace standardized primitives in a production profile.

### 11.2 Trust boundary

Research baseline: HKDF-SHA-256 with distinct documented context labels; AES-256-GCM-SIV per RFC 8452; canonical versioned context metadata authenticated as associated data; explicit lifecycle states for establishment, derivation, sealing, verification, rotation, rejection and recovery. Optional ML-KEM integration must use a published conformant construction and specified key combiner. Do not invent a hybrid combiner. Review current FIPS 203 errata and SP 800-227 before implementation. RFC 9936 (March 2026) specifies ML-KEM use in CMS but does not specify every Titan envelope format.

### 11.3 Merlin module

The new module python/merlin/titan_crypto_lab.py defines a constrained profile allow-list, models lifecycle states and fail-closed scenarios, and emits a deterministic research matrix for context binding, hybrid KEM integration, key lifecycle and interoperability. It performs no cryptographic operations. It is not a security proof, entropy test, side-channel assessment or certification.

### 11.4 Standards

- NIST FIPS 203 ML-KEM: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final
- NIST SP 800-227: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/227/final
- RFC 8452 AES-GCM-SIV: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8452
- RFC 5869 HKDF: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5869
- RFC 9936 ML-KEM in CMS: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9936

### 11.5 Promotion gates

Require exact artifact digests, reproducible vectors and negative tests, independent implementation interoperability, formal claims with explicit assumptions, independent cryptographic review, CACE verification of artifact identity/provenance, and human approval. No generated design, graph complexity, benchmark, simulation or unit-test count is a security proof.
