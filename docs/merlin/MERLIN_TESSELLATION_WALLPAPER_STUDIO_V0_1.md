# Merlin Studio B: Tessellation, Ornament and Wallpaper Symmetry v0.1

**Status:** curriculum specification; not yet a validated learning intervention.

## Purpose
Learners discover how local rules create global pattern. They make a tiling by hand, identify transformations that preserve it, test whether a proposed repeat is possible, and build a generative version. Artistic and mathematical outcomes are assessed independently.

## Learning sequence
1. **Look before naming.** Compare floor tiles, textiles, mosaics, quilts, brickwork and natural patterns. Record repeated motifs, gaps, boundaries, scale changes and deliberate irregularities.
2. **Build a tile physically.** Use paper cut-and-slide, edge matching, tracing and transparent overlays. Ask what must happen to one edge for the opposite edge to fit.
3. **Move the motif.** Explore translation, rotation, reflection and glide reflection. Distinguish symmetry of a motif from symmetry of the entire repeating pattern.
4. **Test the plane.** Classify wallpaper symmetries through allowed combinations of translations and point symmetries. Introduce the 17 wallpaper groups only after learners can explain transformations in their own constructions.
5. **Formalize.** Represent transformations as maps; compose them; test closure, identity, inverses and associativity on finite examples. Connect lattice vectors to coordinates and matrices.
6. **Generate and perturb.** Code a repeatable tile; vary motif, lattice, color, scale and controlled defects. Make artistic intention explicit.
7. **Critique and transfer.** Compare a mathematically valid but visually weak tiling with a compelling one; transfer the same rules to fabric, architecture, animation or 3D surfaces.

## Mathematical spine
- Euclidean isometries: translation, rotation, reflection, glide reflection.
- Lattices and fundamental domains.
- Group actions, generators, composition and invariants.
- Crystallographic restriction and wallpaper groups.
- Modular arithmetic and periodicity.
- Later extensions: frieze groups, orbifolds, quasicrystals, aperiodic tilings, hyperbolic tilings and symmetry on curved surfaces.

Do not present the 17 wallpaper groups as a memorization list. Derive constraints from lattice preservation and test candidate transformations.

## Studio artifacts and assessment
Each learner keeps source observations and attribution; hand-built tile; transformation table; proof or counterexample for a claimed symmetry; generative source code or reproducible construction; final artwork; revision note.

**Mathematical rubric:** correct transformations, composition, lattice reasoning, invariants, proof/test quality and transfer.

**Art rubric:** visual intention, rhythm, balance, contrast, material craft, controlled variation, cultural/contextual interpretation and revision.

A beautiful image is not proof of symmetry. A valid group action is not proof of artistic success. Keep the judgments separate and allow human review for artistic quality and cultural interpretation.

## Hermes routing
Recommend this studio when evidence shows readiness in motif construction, coordinate reasoning or basic transformations. Route learners back when they confuse motif symmetry with pattern symmetry. Store attempts as append-only evidence with task version, rubric version, artifact hash and session timestamp. Never infer mastery from viewing the lesson or exporting an image.
