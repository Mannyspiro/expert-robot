# Problem-directed Ideonomy and lacuna search

1. Name a blockage, unexplained observation, trade-off or material gap.
2. Declare objective, baseline, assumptions, constraints and useful evidence.
3. Choose meaningful axes: mechanism, function, representation, scale, substrate, constraint, measurement or verification. Keep stable primitive IDs, prerequisites, costs, maturity, explicit pair compatibility and higher-order constraints.
4. Count the finite declared space before filtering. Use bounded compatible subsets, a morphological matrix, constrained search or measured t-way coverage where useful. Cap examined combinations as well as returned candidates.
5. Preserve method/order/seed and examined, rejected, duplicate, returned, output-omitted and unexamined counts. State the space each describes. A truncated lexicographic prefix is not representative or diversity-optimal.
6. For each selected candidate retain mechanism, assumptions, novelty=unknown, predicted observable, falsifier, cheapest prototype, resource estimate, dependencies and independent-check route. Pairwise compatibility only admits consideration.
7. Check the existing idea/co-occurrence ledger and relevant literature. Define a lacuna relative to that ledger/version, not global knowledge.
8. For alternative or alien models, perturb explicit axioms, sensors, scales or representations. Separate mathematical consistency from empirical feasibility. Do not privilege or reject unfamiliar ideas solely for unfamiliarity.
9. Preserve rejected/null candidates with reasons. A failure narrows the declared model space; it does not prove universal impossibility.

NIST interaction testing provides a useful bounded-search analogy. Its coverage concerns specified factors and interaction strengths, not all faults or discoveries. See [SP800-142](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/142/final) and [IR7878](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/7878/final).
